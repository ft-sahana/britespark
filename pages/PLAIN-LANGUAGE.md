# Plain-language rewrite — five worst questions

The original form (HSP-1, rev. 03/2019) uses department vocabulary
throughout — legal citations, technical benefits terms, and phrasing no
applicant would use about their own life. Below are the five worst
offenders, chosen because they either appear on our four rebuilt pages
or were flagged directly in the persona notes (Denise: "she does not know
what a 'gross periodic emolument' is").

---

### 1. Wages question (page 4 — Income)

**Original:**
> "Declare all gross periodic emoluments received by any member of the
> household, before deductions other than tax and mandatory contributions."

**Rebuilt:**
> "Wages from a job"
> *Hint: Before tax and National Insurance are taken off — the amount on
> your payslip before deductions.*

**Why:** This is the exact phrase the persona notes call out as the
specific term that defeats Denise. "Emolument" is legal/HR vocabulary
with no equivalent in everyday speech — she earns money cleaning offices
and has no way to know whether that counts.

---

### 2. Self-employment income (page 4 — Income)

**Original:**
> "Net profit from self-employment, computed in accordance with §6.5"

**Rebuilt:**
> "Self-employment profit"
> *Hint: Your income after business expenses, before tax.*

**Why:** A manual section reference is meaningless without the manual in
hand — and the brief notes the manual itself changes quarterly. The
rebuilt hint states the calculation in plain terms instead of pointing
to a document the applicant doesn't have.

---

### 3. Household composition question (page 3 — Household)

**Original:**
> "List every person who ordinarily resides with you and shares living
> arrangements, excluding persons holding a separate tenancy or licence
> with no shared living arrangements (see Manual §1.4.3)."

**Rebuilt:**
> "Who else lives with you?"
> *Hint: Include anyone who shares your home and day-to-day living
> arrangements — family, partner, or anyone else. You don't need to
> include a lodger who has their own separate tenancy agreement.*

**Why:** The original is one 40-word sentence with an embedded legal
exception and a citation. The rebuild states the common case first in
plain language, then covers the edge case (a lodger) in one short
sentence — without requiring the applicant to parse a citation to
understand the question.

---

### 4. Department reference number (page 1 — Applicant details)

**Original:**
> "Client Identifier (if previously known to the Department)"

**Rebuilt:**
> "Department reference number (optional)"
> *Hint: Only if you've applied to this office before and have a
> reference number. Most first-time applicants don't have one — leave
> this blank.*

**Why:** Called out directly in the persona notes: Denise sees "Client
Identifier" on the very first field of the form and assumes she's in the
wrong place. This is a first-page abandonment risk before she's answered
a single question. The rebuild states plainly that most first-time
applicants won't have one, which is the reassurance she actually needs.

---

### 5. Capital and resources intro (page 5 — Savings and property)

**Original:**
> "Declare all countable resources held by the household, net of
> encumbrances. Disregarded resources are listed at §2.4.2 and need not
> be declared."

**Rebuilt:**
> "Tell us what savings, property, or other assets your household has.
> You don't need to include the home you live in, or everyday personal
> belongings."

**Why:** "Countable resources... net of encumbrances" and a citation to
a disregard list the applicant doesn't have access to. The rebuild names
the two most common disregards in plain language (your home, everyday
belongings) instead of pointing to a manual section.

---

## What this doesn't fix

This document covers five questions on the four rebuilt pages. The
original form has 129 fields across eight pages — plenty of jargon
remains on pages 2, 6, 7, and 8, which are out of scope for this rebuild.
The stronger version of this deliverable, per the brief's "if you have
time" section, would be reducing the question count itself rather than
just rewording — several fields here (e.g. "maintenance received in
respect of an adult" vs "for a child") could arguably be merged into one
question with a follow-up, which we didn't have time to redesign.
